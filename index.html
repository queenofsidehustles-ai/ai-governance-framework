First — your context

Risk isn't the same for every business. These three answers change how your results are scored and prioritized.

Pillar 1 — Clarity

You cannot govern what you cannot see. This pillar establishes whether you know what is actually running.

Do you have a written list of every AI tool in use across your organization?
Including tools individual staff signed up for without approval.
Can you name which AI tools influence decisions that affect a customer, an employee, or money?HIGH RISK
This is the difference between a convenience tool and a governance obligation.
Do your team members know when they are using AI versus their own judgment on a task?
Undisclosed AI use inside a team is how errors become invisible.

Pillar 2 — Accountability

When AI produces a bad outcome, the first question anyone asks is who was responsible. This pillar determines whether you have an answer.

Is there one named person accountable for each AI tool's output?HIGH RISK
Not a team. Not a department. A name.
Is there a written AI usage policy your staff have actually read and acknowledged?
An unacknowledged policy provides no protection.
If an AI tool produced a damaging output tomorrow, does your team know who to tell and what happens next?
Incident response written down beats incident response invented under pressure.

Pillar 3 — Data Integrity

AI is confident regardless of whether its inputs are correct. This pillar tests whether you have verified what goes in and where it goes.

Do you know whether your AI tools store, share, or train on the data you put into them?HIGH RISK
Client information, employee records, and proprietary work all count.
Is the data your AI tools rely on verified as current and accurate?
Outdated inputs produce confidently wrong outputs at scale.
Do you have rules about what information staff may not put into an AI tool?
Most data exposure happens through ordinary staff use, not a breach.

Pillar 4 — Risk Awareness

Every AI tool will be wrong eventually. This pillar tests whether you know where that would hurt most.

Have you identified which AI-influenced decisions would cause the most damage if they were wrong?
Not all errors are equal. Governance effort should follow consequence.
Is there a human review step before AI output reaches a customer, a regulator, or a hiring decision?HIGH RISK
Human oversight on high-stakes output is the single highest-value control.
Do you have a plan for what you would do and say publicly if an AI failure became visible to your customers?
The response window is hours, not weeks.

Pillar 5 — Documentation

Documentation is what converts good intentions into defensible evidence. This is also the backbone of any management-system standard, including ISO/IEC 42001.

Do you keep records of which AI tools are used, who owns them, and what data they touch?
One maintained page beats a perfect policy nobody wrote down.
If an enterprise client or auditor asked for your AI governance documentation this week, could you produce it?HIGH RISK
Increasingly this question appears in procurement and vendor review, not just audits.
Do you revisit your AI practices on a set schedule as tools and staff change?
A framework written once and never revisited stops being true within months.
